## 1. The War Australia Lost to Birds
- **Hook:** "In 1932, Australia sent soldiers with machine guns to fight birds. The birds won."
- **Twist:** A politician joked that the emus were the ones who deserved medals.
- **Why it animates well:** Emus scattering in every direction, a truck bouncing over rocks, soldiers staring at an empty field.
- **True:** Documented (Britannica, National Geographic). Never show birds being hurt: the gag is that they always escape.

## 2. The Warship That Sank Before Leaving Harbour
- **Hook:** "Sweden built the mightiest warship in the world. It sank in about twenty minutes."
- **Twist:** Before launch, the admiral had sailors run from side to side as a test, and it nearly capsized. He sent the ship out anyway.
- **Why it animates well:** Crowds cheering on the quay, a gust tipping the ship, water rushing into the gunports, only the mast tips left.
- **True:** Documented (Vasa Museum, Britannica): sank ~1,300 m into her maiden voyage on 10 Aug 1628. Don't dwell on the ~30 deaths.

## 3. The War That Saved the Leaning Tower
- **Hook:** "The Leaning Tower of Pisa only exists because the builders went off to war."
- **Twist:** The pause lasted almost a century, and the ground got time to settle. Without the war, the tower would have fallen.
- **Why it animates well:** The tower tilting floor by floor, builders dropping their tools, a time-lapse of seasons over a half-built stump.
- **True:** Documented (Britannica, History.com, PBS NOVA).

## 4. The Bear Who Became a Corporal
- **Hook:** "One soldier in the Polish army had a rank, a service number, and fur."
- **Twist:** He was promoted for hauling crates of shells, but when there was no battle he looked for the empty ones to carry.
- **Why it animates well:** A cub drinking from a bottle, a huge bear stacking crates onto a truck, a salute with a paw.
- **True, one disputed detail:** Wojtek and his rank are documented; hedge the crate-carrying ("soldiers said…").

## 5. The Day It Rained Meat in Kentucky
- **Hook:** "On a clear, sunny morning in 1876, it rained meat."
- **Twist:** The best explanation is a flock of vultures throwing up all at once. Some locals had already tasted it.
- **Why it animates well:** Chunks plopping into a farmer's yard, a puzzled taste test, the camera tilting up to queasy vultures.
- **Disputed:** The event is documented (NYT 1876, Scientific American); the cause is not certain — say "the best explanation".

## 6. The Chocolate Bar That Invented the Microwave
- **Hook:** "The microwave oven was invented by a melted chocolate bar."
- **Twist:** Next he tried popcorn kernels, and they popped all over the lab.
- **Why it animates well:** A glowing radar tube, a chocolate stain spreading on a pocket, popcorn exploding everywhere.
- **Mostly true:** Percy Spencer / Raytheon is documented (IEEE Spectrum, Lemelson-MIT); the candy-bar moment is the popular version — hedge it.

## 7. The Smell That Built London's Sewers
- **Hook:** "London's sewers exist because politicians couldn't stand the smell."
- **Twist:** Years of warnings changed nothing. One hot summer under Parliament's windows did.
- **Why it animates well:** Green stink clouds over the river, lawmakers with handkerchiefs, curtains dunked in buckets, a giant tunnel being dug.
- **True:** Documented (London Museum, Historic England): the Great Stink of 1858.

## 8. The Bath That Caught a Thief
- **Hook:** "A king's crown was solved by a man getting into his bathtub."
- **Twist:** He was so excited that he ran through the streets shouting "Eureka!" and forgot his clothes.
- **Why it animates well:** A wobbling golden crown, water splashing over the tub, a naked run that cuts away at the last second (never show nudity).
- **Legend:** Told by Vitruvius ~200 years later — open with "Legend says…".

## 9. The Messy Desk That Saved Millions
- **Hook:** "The most important medicine in history was found because a scientist didn't clean up."
- **Twist:** He came back from holiday and found that mould on a dish he'd left out had killed the bacteria around it. It was penicillin.
- **Why it animates well:** A chaotic lab bench, a dirty dish under a magnifying glass, mould pushing bacteria back like an army.
- **True:** Alexander Fleming, 1928 (well documented). Leave out the debated open-window detail.
