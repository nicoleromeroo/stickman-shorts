## slot4 — The War That Saved the Leaning Tower

The Leaning Tower of Pisa is only still standing because its builders went off to war.
Italy, eleven seventy-three. Pisa starts building a grand bell tower.
But the ground is soft clay and sand, and the foundation is only three metres deep.
By the third floor, the tower is already leaning.
Then Pisa goes to war, and the builders put down their tools.
Guess how long the pause lasts. Comment a number.
Almost a hundred years.
And all that time, the soil under the tower slowly settles and gets firmer.
When work starts again, the builders try to fix the lean by building the new floors at an angle.
It still leans. But experts say, without that long pause, it would almost certainly have toppled.
So the war that stopped the tower is the reason it's still standing.

## slot2 — The Day It Rained Meat in Kentucky

On a clear, sunny day in eighteen seventy-six, it rained meat.
Bath County, Kentucky. Mrs. Crouch is outside making soap.
Out of a perfectly clear sky, flakes start falling, like large snowflakes.
Except these snowflakes are chunks of meat.
They cover the fences and the ground, across an area the size of a football field.
Two brave local men taste it. Their verdict: mutton, or maybe venison.
Guess before the end: where did it come from?
Scientists find lung tissue, muscle and cartilage.
So what was it doing in the sky?
The best explanation came from a local doctor: vultures.
When one vulture gets scared and throws up, the rest of the flock often joins in.
So the meat probably came from a flock of vomiting vultures. And two men had already tasted it.

## slot3 — The Chocolate Bar That Invented the Microwave

The microwave oven was invented thanks to a melted chocolate bar.
America, nineteen forty-five. Percy Spencer builds magnetrons, the tubes that power radar.
He left school at twelve and taught himself physics.
One day, next to a running magnetron, he notices the chocolate bar in his pocket has melted.
Most people would just wipe their pocket. Percy gets curious.
He holds popcorn kernels next to the tube. They pop all over the lab.
Guess what he tried next.
An egg. It burst.
You see, microwaves make the water in food spin, and that spinning makes heat.
Two years later, the first microwave oven goes on sale. It's nearly six feet tall.
And its name? The Radarange. A radar machine, built to cook your dinner.
