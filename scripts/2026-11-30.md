## slot4 — The War That Saved the Leaning Tower

The Leaning Tower of Pisa is still standing because its builders went to war.
Italy, eleven seventy-three. Pisa starts a grand bell tower.
But the ground is soft clay and sand.
By the third floor, it's already leaning.
Then Pisa goes to war, and the builders leave.
Guess how long they're gone.
Almost a hundred years.
And all that time, the soil underneath settles and gets firmer.
Experts say without that pause, the tower would almost certainly have toppled.
So a war is the reason it's still standing.

## slot2 — The Day It Rained Meat in Kentucky

On a clear, sunny day in eighteen seventy-six, it rained meat.
In Kentucky, Mrs. Crouch is outside making soap.
Out of a clear sky, chunks of meat start falling, like snowflakes.
They cover a whole field.
Two brave men taste it. Their verdict: mutton, or maybe venison.
Guess where it came from.
Scientists find lung tissue, muscle and cartilage.
The best explanation came from a local doctor: vultures.
When one vulture throws up, the rest of the flock often joins in.
So it was probably vulture vomit. And two men had already tasted it.

## slot3 — The Chocolate Bar That Invented the Microwave

The microwave oven was invented thanks to a melted chocolate bar.
Nineteen forty-five. Percy Spencer builds magnetrons, the tubes that power radar.
One day, next to a running magnetron, the chocolate in his pocket melts.
Most people would wipe their pocket. Percy gets curious.
He holds popcorn kernels near the tube. They pop all over the lab.
Guess what he tried next.
An egg. It burst.
You see, microwaves make the water in food spin, and that makes heat.
Two years later, the first microwave oven goes on sale. It's nearly six feet tall.
Its name? The Radarange.
